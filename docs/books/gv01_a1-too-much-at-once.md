## Remember

### Page 17 — Śloka 1.28

<!-- verse-source: lib/data/chapters/chapter1.js -->

कृपया परयाविष्टो विषीदन्निदमब्रवीत् अर्जुन उवाच दृष्ट्वेमं स्वजनं कृष्ण युयुत्सुं समुपस्थितम्

`kṛpayā parayāviṣṭo viṣīdannidamabravīt arjuna uvāca dṛṣṭvemaṃ svajanaṃ kṛṣṇa yuyutsuṃ samupasthitam`

**Child meaning:** Arjuna tells Krishna, "When I see everyone I care about ready to fight, my heart feels heavy. My legs feel shaky and my mouth feels dry. Even a hero can feel very, very nervous."

### Page 18 — Śloka 1.30

<!-- verse-source: lib/data/chapters/chapter1.js -->

गाण्डीवं स्रंसते हस्तात्त्वक्चैव परिदह्यते न च शक्नोम्यवस्थातुं भ्रमतीव च मे मनः

`gāṇḍīvaṃ sraṃsate hastāttvakcaiva paridahyate na ca śaknomyavasthātuṃ bhramatīva ca me manaḥ`

**Child meaning:** The bow Gandiva slips from my hand and my skin is burning. I can no longer stand. My mind seems to be reeling.

### Page 19 — Remember line

In the Bhagavad Gita, from India's great puranas, a warrior stands where he must act — but first his body speaks. His skin burns. His mind reels. He cannot see a good path ahead, and he will not pretend otherwise. Naming that honest overwhelm is not the end of courage. Sometimes it is where clear thinking can begin.

## Practice

### Page 20

**Name the pile** — Draw three small boxes. In each box, write or draw one thing that feels "too much" today. Circle just **one** box to handle first.

### Page 21

**One thing first** — With a grown-up, pick one tiny job (one dish, one page, one breath). Finish it before starting the next.

### Page 22

**Ask beside you** — Practice saying: "This is too much for me right now. Will you help me pick one thing?" Say it to a mirror, then to someone you trust.

## For grown-ups

### Page 24

**The Problem · Feeling overwhelmed**

Children often meet several stressors at once — social, sensory, and task demands stacking before they have language for it. Vanshi's arc mirrors a common pattern: high empathy + high responsibility → shutdown or sharp words. Naming "too much" without shame, then shrinking the task list, builds regulation skills that generalize beyond one party setup.

**Discussion:** "When did your body last feel too full? What is one small yes you could choose today?"

**Verse context (optional):** Bhagavad Gita 1.28–1.30 — Arjuna's physical and emotional overwhelm before dialogue begins.
