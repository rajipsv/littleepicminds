## Story

### Pages 2–4 — Opens

Sunrise painted Courage Hill gold. Mimi the rabbit stood at the bottom with the opening ribbon in her paws — and every butterfly in her tummy wanted to fly away.

Below the hill, Spring Fair hummed. Flags snapped. Friends called names. In Blossom Meadow, the red cloth had come off the great drum — and someone was testing it: *BOOM.*

Mimi’s ears snapped toward the sound. Her paws shook.

“Almost time,” called Gulu from the sign-up table. “Ribbon holder to the hill!”

### Pages 5–6 — Honest body

Mimi looked at the ribbon — soft Grove green, gold edge, heavier than she expected.

Her tummy flipped. Flip-flip-flip.

Her paws shook. Shake-shake-shake.

Kiki flew overhead. “You’ll be great! Everyone’s watching!”

That did not help.

Bobo waved the team banner from the path. “Race you up!”

Mimi’s legs felt like wet noodles.

### Pages 7–8 — Behind Timo

Mimi backed up until she bumped something hard and round.

Timo the tortoise.

“Oh,” Mimi whispered. “Good. Hide me.”

She tried to tuck herself behind his shell. Timo was wide, but Mimi’s ribbon stuck out.

“You are not hidden,” Timo said gently. “Your paws are shaking on my back.”

“I know,” Mimi said. Her voice came out small. “My whole body is scared. I don’t want everyone looking.”

Timo was quiet for a moment. Then he said, “My legs feel slow when I am scared. I do not hide them. I notice them.”

### Pages 9–10 — Smell the flower

“Want a game?” Timo asked.

Mimi nodded — anything before the hill.

Timo held up one claw. “Smell the flower.”

Mimi breathed in through her nose. Slow. She thought of the clover at the hill’s edge.

“Good,” Timo said. “Now blow the candle.”

Mimi breathed out through her mouth. Pffff. Like blowing one birthday candle, not ten.

“Again,” Timo said.

Smell the flower. Blow the candle.

The butterflies in her tummy did not vanish. They settled — just a little — like birds finding a branch.

“You still look scared,” Timo said.

“I am,” Mimi said. Honest. “My paws still shake.”

“Mine too, inside,” Timo said. “We can walk anyway.”

### Pages 11–12 — One step

The path up Courage Hill had twelve stone steps. Mimi could see each one.

Step one — her paw trembled on the rock.

Step two — the drum boomed again from the meadow. *BOOM.*

Mimi stopped. Tummy flip.

“Say it,” Timo called from below. He had not climbed. He was waiting — not pulling.

“My tummy is fluttery,” Mimi said out loud. No one else had to hear. But she heard.

“And?” Timo asked.

“And I’m still going,” Mimi said.

### Pages 13–15 — Resolution

At the top, the whole Grove spread out — tents, streamers, faces turned up.

Mimi’s paws shook so hard the ribbon rustled.

Guru Ma Owl sat on the highest stone, not saying a word. Just watching with kind eyes.

Mimi held the ribbon with both paws. Not perfect. Not still. **Holding.**

The crowd grew quiet.

Mimi lifted the ribbon. The green and gold caught the sun.

Below, Kiki cheered. Bobo whooped. Gulu clapped. Pip peeked from behind a flower crate — brave in their own small way.

The drum waited in the meadow. The fair had opened.

Mimi’s tummy still fluttered. Her paws still shook.

She had told the truth about her body — and walked up anyway.

That felt like its own kind of honest.

## Moral

You can tell the truth about a scared body — shaky paws and fluttery tummies are allowed. You can still take the next step.

**Child repeat line:** "I can name what my body feels."

---

## Practice activities

1. **Body check** — Before something big, put a paw on your chest or tummy. What do you feel? Say it out loud: *"My heart is fast"* or *"My legs feel wobbly."*
2. **Smell the flower, blow the candle** — With a grown-up, breathe in slow (smell the flower), breathe out slow (blow one candle). Try three times.
3. **Twelve steps** — Count twelve steps across a room. Pause on step two and step eight. Keep going anyway.
